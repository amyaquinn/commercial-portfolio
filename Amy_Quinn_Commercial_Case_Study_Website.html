01 / The context

Working at the heart of national accounts.

During my 12-month placement, I supported L’Oréal Ireland’s Dermatological Beauty National Accounts team across La Roche-Posay, Vichy and CeraVe, primarily in the pharmacy channel.

The role connected commercial reporting with the practical realities of retail: orders, stock availability, promotions, launches and customer communication.

I also gained exposure to the division’s developing presence in grocery retail, learning how approaches vary across pharmacy, e-commerce and new channels.

02 / Supporting growth

From insight to action.

Growth isn’t one big moment. It comes from understanding the market, spotting operational risks and making sure commercial plans actually reach customers.

01 / ANALYSE

Commercial performance

Reviewed sell-out, market share, stock and promotional performance to identify trends, availability risks and potential opportunities.

02 / INFORM

Decision-ready reporting

Prepared recurring sales, market and competitor reports to support account planning and internal conversations.

03 / COORDINATE

Launches and presell

Worked with demand planning and commercial stakeholders to align stock availability, timing and promotional activity.

04 / EXECUTE

Retailer-facing delivery

Supported product information, retailer briefs and point-of-sale coordination so plans could translate into accurate execution.

03 / Taking ownership

Two e-commerce accounts. Real responsibility.

When a colleague went on maternity leave, I took responsibility for day-to-day management of Millies and Cloud10.

Each was an established account generating approximately €20,000 in monthly revenue during the period I supported it.

My focus was continuity, communication and making sure commercial activity kept moving despite competing demands.

01

Own the communication

Act as a day-to-day retailer contact and follow through on queries and requests.

02

Prioritise what matters

Monitor orders, stock availability, promotions and launch deadlines, responding to urgent issues first.

03

Bring teams together

Coordinate with account, brand, demand planning and inventory colleagues to resolve blockers.

04

Stay commercially aware

Track account priorities and revenue targets while maintaining accurate information for retailers.

04 / The takeaway

What the experience taught me.

Commercial growth depends on more than a good plan. It takes reliable insight, customer relationships and consistent execution across teams.

I left the placement more confident in taking ownership, balancing competing priorities and using data to support decisions. It confirmed my interest in building a career in FMCG sales, commercial strategy and customer account management.
